Educational tool, not financial advice. SMC/ICT concepts (order blocks, fair value gaps, liquidity sweeps, BOS/CHoCH) and the Elliott Wave count are pattern-recognition heuristics applied here mechanically to historical candles — they are not a proven predictive edge, and institutional intent is inferred, not observed. Wave counting in particular is inherently subjective in real Elliott Wave practice (alternate counts are normal); the count shown here is one simplified, rules-checked reading of the last six swing points, not an authoritative label. Markers can repaint as new candles close. Crypto data comes from Binance's public feed and streams tick-by-tick over WebSocket when Live is on. Forex and index data comes from Twelve Data using your own free API key, refreshed by polling roughly every 20 seconds — free-tier Twelve Data quotes are typically delayed 1–15 minutes, not true real-time, and index/forex symbol coverage varies by their data provider. Always backtest and manage risk independently of any signal shown here. Once a BUY/SELL fires, it stays locked (SL and all four TP levels don't move) until it fully resolves — 25% of the simulated position scales out at each TP level as price reaches it, with the remainder exiting at SL or TP4; only price movement observed after the trade opened counts toward a hit, so a wick that happened before the signal fired can't retroactively trigger it. The next call only appears after full resolution, and the trade log is a simple simulated record of that scale-out on this page — it isn't a real position, executed order, or verified track record. The optional AI signal sends the already-computed structure summary (not raw candles) to Anthropic's API using your own API key entered above, and asks a language model to weigh it into a single read; it is a second opinion generated from the same mechanical detections shown elsewhere on this page, not an independent market analysis, and it can be wrong, inconsistent between runs, or unavailable if the key is missing or rate-limited. Each click is a billed API call against your own key. Higher-timeframe confluence is a courtesy check (one extra fetch per scan, refreshed every 2 minutes) — it doesn't block a call, only adds a caution note and caps confidence at 50% when the higher timeframe disagrees. The kill zone badge is UTC-clock-based session labeling only, not a liquidity measurement, and crypto trades 24/7 so "low" tier there is a rough guide, not a rule. The Backtest button replays this exact detection and scale-out logic candle-by-candle over whatever window is currently loaded (typically ~200 candles) with no look-ahead — it's a quick sanity check on this symbol/timeframe's recent behavior, not a robust multi-year backtest, and past performance here says nothing about future results. The position size calculator is arithmetic only (risk ÷ SL distance) — it doesn't know your actual exchange's lot/contract rules, leverage, or fees. The Screener runs the same one-shot mechanical read across a fixed watchlist of major crypto pairs (it does not open or track live trades for them) — it's a way to see where setups currently exist, not a ranked recommendation of what to trade, and results go stale the moment you stop re-running it. None of the statistics on this page — win rate, profit factor, drawdown, Kelly sizing, or otherwise — come from a licensed, audited, or regulated source; they're arithmetic on this page's own simulated trades. This entire tool remains an educational pattern-recognition aid, not financial, investment, or trading advice, and nothing on this page should be read as a personal recommendation — it doesn't know your finances, goals, or risk tolerance the way a licensed advisor would.
